Planning the week takes 20 minutes and can save a lot of last-minute takeaways and half-used bags of salad. Here's a simple routine that works for most families.

## 1. Start with what you've got

Check the fridge, freezer and cupboards first. Anything close to its date goes into Monday or Tuesday's meals. A half bag of spinach can go into a curry; leftover yoghurt makes a quick raita or marinade.

## 2. Decide the shape of the week

- **Busy weeknights:** pick dinners you can cook in your real time limit – 15, 30 or 45 minutes.
- **Cook once, eat twice:** a slow cooker chilli or a big traybake covers two nights. Cool leftovers quickly, keep them in the fridge and eat them within two days.
- **Weekends:** a longer cook, like a roast, and its leftovers become Monday's sandwiches.

## 3. Write one shopping list, by aisle

Group the list the way the shop is laid out: fruit & veg, meat & fish, dairy, cupboard, frozen and bakery. Combine duplicates (two recipes needing an onion = one bag). You'll spend less time in the shop and buy fewer extras.

## 4. Keep allergens out – and still read the labels

UK food businesses must tell you if a food contains any of the **14 major allergens**: celery, cereals containing gluten (wheat, rye, barley and oats), crustaceans, eggs, fish, lupin, milk, molluscs, mustard, tree nuts, peanuts, sesame, soya, and sulphur dioxide/sulphites. Source: [GOV.UK – food allergen information businesses must provide](https://www.gov.uk/food-allergies/information-businesses-provide), checked November 2026.

Allergens hide in everyday ingredients: pesto (often nuts and milk), soy sauce (soya and usually wheat), houmous (sesame), Worcestershire sauce (fish), mayonnaise (egg) and dried fruit (often sulphites). Recipes and brands change, so **always check ingredient labels** when you shop. If someone in your family has a serious allergy, follow the advice of their doctor or dietitian.

## 5. Set a budget and check it

Add a rough price next to each item and total it up. If you're over, swap one meat dinner for a bean or lentil one, use frozen veg, or choose own-brand.
