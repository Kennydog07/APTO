If your flight left from a UK airport, or arrived in the UK on a UK or EU airline, you're probably covered by **UK261** – the UK version of the EU's flight compensation rules. Here is how it works, in plain English.

## How much can you claim?

Compensation is a **fixed amount per passenger**, based on the flight distance. It has nothing to do with what you paid for the ticket.

| Flight distance                                    | Compensation                                |
| -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| 1,500 km or less (e.g. Glasgow to Amsterdam)       | £220                                        |
| 1,500 – 3,500 km (e.g. East Midlands to Marrakesh) | £350                                        |
| Over 3,500 km (e.g. London to New York)            | £520 (£260 if you arrive 3 to 4 hours late) |

Source: the [Civil Aviation Authority's delays page](https://www.caa.co.uk/passengers-and-public/resolving-travel-problems/delays-and-cancellations/delays/). Flights covered only by the EU rules pay €250, €400 or €600.

## When do you qualify?

- **Delays:** you reach your final destination **3 hours or more late**. It's the arrival time that counts, not the departure.
- **Cancellations:** you were told **less than 14 days** before departure. If the airline re-routed you on a flight close to your original times, the amount can be reduced or removed – the [CAA cancellations page](https://www.caa.co.uk/passengers-and-public/resolving-travel-problems/delays-and-cancellations/cancellations/) sets out the exact windows.
- **Denied boarding:** you were bumped from an overbooked flight and didn't volunteer.

Airlines don't have to pay if the disruption was caused by **extraordinary circumstances** such as severe weather or air traffic control restrictions. Ordinary technical faults usually don't count. If an airline says the cause was extraordinary, it's reasonable to ask it for evidence.

Separately from compensation, the airline must **look after you** while you wait: food and drink, and a hotel if you're stuck overnight. If it didn't, keep receipts and claim the reasonable cost back.

## How to claim

1. **Claim directly from the airline.** Most have an online form. You don't need a claims company, which would take a cut.
2. **Put it in writing**: flight number, date, booking reference, every passenger's name, what happened, the amount per passenger and any expenses.
3. **Be firm and polite.** Ask for payment and a written reply within a reasonable time.
4. **If they refuse, or don't reply within 8 weeks**, escalate to the airline's Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) scheme, or the CAA if the airline isn't in one.

You can claim for flights up to **6 years** ago in England, Wales and Northern Ireland, or **5 years** in Scotland.
