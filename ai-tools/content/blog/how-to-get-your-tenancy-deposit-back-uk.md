Moved out and still waiting for your deposit? Or has your landlord sent a long list of deductions? Here's what the rules say, and how to ask for your money back in writing.

## First: was your deposit protected?

Landlords must put your deposit in a government-approved protection scheme:

| Where            | Deadline to protect it                       | Schemes                                                                         |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| England          | 30 days of getting it                        | Deposit Protection Service, mydeposits, Tenancy Deposit Scheme                  |
| Wales            | 30 days (with the required information)      | Same three schemes                                                              |
| Scotland         | 30 working days of the tenancy starting      | Letting Protection Service Scotland, mydeposits Scotland, SafeDeposits Scotland |
| Northern Ireland | 28 days (written information within 35 days) | TDS Northern Ireland, My Deposits Northern Ireland                              |

Sources: [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/tenancy-deposit-protection), [GOV.WALES](https://www.gov.wales/deposits-guidance-tenants), [mygov.scot](https://www.mygov.scot/tenant-deposits/protection), [nidirect](https://www.nidirect.gov.uk/articles/tenancy-deposit-scheme-information-tenants). Checked November 2026.

You can check by contacting the schemes with your name, the address and the tenancy dates.

## How long does the landlord have to give it back?

- **England:** within **10 days** of you both agreeing how much you'll get back.
- **Wales:** there's no fixed legal deadline, but GOV.WALES says it usually takes 5 to 10 days.
- **Scotland and Northern Ireland:** the deposit is repaid through the scheme at the end of the tenancy.

## Challenging deductions

Your landlord can take money for things like damage, unpaid rent or missing items. They shouldn't charge for **fair wear and tear**: the normal marks and wear that come from living somewhere. The best evidence is:

- the check-in inventory and check-out report
- dated photos or video from when you moved in and out
- receipts, for example for professional cleaning
- emails or messages with the landlord or agent

If you can't agree, every scheme has a **free dispute resolution service**. In England both sides have to agree to use it, and its decision is final.

## If your deposit wasn't protected

- **England and Wales:** you can apply to the county court, which can order the landlord to pay between 1 and 3 times the deposit.
- **Scotland:** you can apply to the First-tier Tribunal (Housing and Property Chamber) during the tenancy or up to 3 months after it ends. It can award up to 3 times the deposit, and it's free to apply.
- **Northern Ireland:** contact the Environmental Health Office at your council, which can fine the landlord 3 times the deposit.

Get free advice from Citizens Advice or Shelter before going to court or a tribunal.
