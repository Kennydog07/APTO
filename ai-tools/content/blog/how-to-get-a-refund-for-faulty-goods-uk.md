Shops often say "contact the manufacturer" or "no refunds after 14 days". Usually they're wrong. Here's what UK law actually gives you.

## Faulty goods: it depends on when you got it

| Time since delivery           | What you can ask for                                                                                                           |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Up to 30 days                 | A full refund (the "short-term right to reject")                                                                               |
| 30 days to 6 months           | A repair or replacement. The fault is presumed to have been there from the start. If that fails, a refund or a price reduction |
| After 6 months                | A repair or replacement, but you may need to show the fault was there at delivery                                              |
| Up to 6 years (5 in Scotland) | The limit for making a claim                                                                                                   |

Your contract is with the **shop**, not the manufacturer. Sources: [Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/consumer-rights/advice/what-do-i-do-if-i-have-a-faulty-product-aTTEK2g0YuEy), [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/accepting-returns-and-giving-refunds), [Consumer Rights Act 2015](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/section/22). Checked November 2026.

## Bought online? You can also change your mind

For most things bought online, by phone or by mail order, you can cancel within **14 days** of receiving the item for any reason, then return it within another 14 days.

## Order never arrived

If no delivery date was agreed, goods should arrive **within 30 days**. If they don't, you can set a final reasonable date, and if it's missed, cancel for a full refund. The goods are the seller's responsibility until they reach you.

## Poor service or workmanship

Services must be carried out with **reasonable care and skill**. If they weren't, you can ask for the work to be put right at no cost, or for a price reduction.

## Wrong energy bill

Complain to your supplier first. If it isn't sorted within **8 weeks**, or they send a "deadlock letter", you can take it to the [Energy Ombudsman](https://www.energyombudsman.org/faqs) for free. Keep paying your usual bills meanwhile.

## How you paid can help

- **Credit card, over £100 and up to £30,000:** you may be able to claim from the card provider under Section 75.
- **Debit card (or credit under £100):** ask your bank for a chargeback.
- **PayPal:** check PayPal Buyer Protection.

See [Citizens Advice](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/consumer/somethings-gone-wrong-with-a-purchase/getting-your-money-back-if-you-paid-by-card-or-paypal/) for details.
